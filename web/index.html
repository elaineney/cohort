Cohort

How a patient joins a clinical trial without handing their chart to the sponsor. Five entries, read top to bottom. Each one names what the ledger learns and what stays in the wallet.

  1. An accredited hospital hashes the patient's chart together with the patient key and a random salt, and appends only that leaf to a historic Merkle tree.

    Biomarker
    Condition code
    Stage
    --

    Private: chart values, patient key, salt.

    Public: one 32-byte leaf.

  2. The sponsor opens a trial with a biomarker window, a target condition, a minimum stage and an age band. The ruleset is checked inside the circuit, never against raw data on chain.

    Biomarker 250 to 500 · condition 704 · stage at least 2 · ages 18 to 75

    --

    Private: nothing about any patient.

    Public: trial id and its rules.

  3. From their own wallet the patient proves the sealed chart sits in the hospital tree and satisfies every rule. The ledger stores an anonymous receipt and a nullifier so the same chart cannot apply twice.

    --

    Private: which leaf, which patient, every value.

    Public: receipt, nullifier, "rules held".

  4. Recruitment closes with a sponsor entropy seed. Each applicant derives a draw ticket from that seed and their receipt, so selection is verifiable and no one can grind for a better ticket.

    --

    Private: the link between ticket and person.

    Public: seed and ticket.

  5. The sponsor accepts a receipt; only the holder of the matching key can confirm. Status moves from applied to accepted to enrolled.

    --

    Private: identity until the clinic visit.

    Public: receipt status.
